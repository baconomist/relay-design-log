# Automated Hottub, research (16 Oct 2026)

Research for the new capstone direction: a cheap automated, IoT-connected hot tub, possibly rented to Airbnb hosts so they can charge more for their stays. The team decided on 15 Oct to pivot away from the festival friend-finder. The old research is in `FYDP Group 2 - PDP research bundle (15 Oct)/`.

## Files

1. **01 Cheapest propane hot tub.md**. The cheapest complete propane tub is the Joolca HOTTUB Kit, C$1,123 on sale. It is a 2-person inflatable with a propane water heater and a 12 V pump.
2. **02 Cheapest hot tub.md**. Inflatables start around US$298 to US$398, or about C$470 in Canada. The cheapest hard-shell tub is about US$2,499. Cheap inflatables are not rated for a Waterloo winter.
3. **03 Winter heating cost - electric vs propane.md**. With propane at C$15 per 20 lb tank, propane (15¢ per kWh of heat) and electricity (10.6¢ to 13.2¢ all-in, 20¢ on-peak) are close, and electricity is a little cheaper. A good tub in regular use costs about C$294 for a November to March season on propane (about 20 tanks) and C$208 to C$260 on electricity. Propane's advantage is speed and not needing a 240 V circuit.
4. **04 Joolca and similar portable tubs.md**. Joolca is a camping tub you fill, heat, soak in and drain. It is small, has no jets, heats unevenly and has no freeze protection. Lucas's home setup (6-person SaluSpa plus pump and shower heater, about $1,100) matches its hardware at the same price.
5. **05 Renting hot tubs to Airbnb hosts.md**. Hot tubs lift winter Airbnb revenue (AirDNA). Travellin' Tubs already rents delivered tubs in Ontario for C$500 a weekend or C$1,080 a month. Waterloo Region exempts private-home spas from public pool rules if a sign is posted.
6. **06 Powering the pump from propane.md**. A thermoelectric generator on the burner could make enough electricity (about 40 W at about 3% efficiency) to run the pump while the tub heats. The heat it doesn't convert still goes into the tub. It can't run a pump all day without overheating the tub, so a battery is still needed. Matters most for off-grid sites. Also compares the quiet ways to make electricity from propane: TEGs, fuel cells, Stirling engines and thermophotovoltaics.
7. **07 Cold weather and heating speed.md**. With the cover off, the SaluSpa's 1.36 kW electric heater can't keep up below about 18°C, because an open tub loses 2 to 5 kW. A cheap 6 L/min, 41,000 BTU propane shower heater (about 9.6 kW into the tub) keeps up easily at -10°C with the cover off, and propane heats the tub about 7 to 9 times faster (about 4 to 5 hours against 30 to 37). Includes the full heat-loss table from -10°C to 20°C.
8. **08 Filling from a lake.md**. A 12 V pump can fill the tub from a lake in about 2.5 hours if it sits within 1.8 m above the water. No permit is needed. The hard parts are sanitizing lake water to hot tub standards, never filling during a blue-green algae bloom, and dechlorinating before draining onto land, never into the lake.
9. **09 Reasons it might not work.md**. The case against, most serious first: app-controlled inflatables already exist, "automated" still needs weekly visits for propane and water, cheap inflatables aren't built for winter or rentals, and no customer has been interviewed yet.

Every source is linked and rated strong, medium or weak, like the PDP bundle.

## Known gaps

- Prices are search snapshots from 16 Oct and were not checked in a cart.
- Heating figures are seller estimates and our own heat-loss maths, not measurements. Logging Lucas's tub with an energy monitor would give real numbers.
- No Airbnb host has been interviewed, so the rental idea is not validated.
- Not researched yet: heat pump heaters, what existing smart hot tub apps can and can't do in detail, insurance and liability for a tub on someone else's property, Airbnb's own hot tub rules, propane heater certification and TSSA rules.
