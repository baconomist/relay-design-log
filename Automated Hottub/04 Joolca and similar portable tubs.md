# Joolca and similar portable tubs

16 Oct 2026

Joolca is the closest product to a cheap propane tub, but it is built for a different job. It is a **camping tub you fill, heat for about 2 hours, soak in and drain**. It is not built to sit outside staying hot, and nothing about it is automated. Its main drawbacks are that it is small, has no jets, heats unevenly, depends on a 12 V pump and good water pressure, and has no freeze protection. Other portable tubs are either electric inflatables that need a plug and are not rated for winter, or wood-fired tubs that cost US$950 to about US$9,500.

| Heat | HOTTAP V2 propane water heater, 37,500 BTU. Water is pumped from the tub through the heater and back. Under 2 hours to soaking temperature. A 20 lb cylinder lasts about 15 hours at full output ([Joolca](https://joolca.com/products/hottub-bundle)) |

Heating 300 L from 15°C to 39°C takes about 8.4 kWh of heat, about 1.5 L of propane at 80% efficiency, so roughly **C$1.30 a fill** with propane at C$15 per 20 lb tank (own calc, using the inputs in 03).

## How close is Joolca to what we want to build?

Close on hardware: an inflatable tub plus a portable propane heater and a pump. Far apart on use:

- Joolca is for one owner taking it camping. Every soak is a manual fill, heat, soak and drain.
- An automated tub (and especially one rented to Airbnbs, see 05) has to stay hot and ready for days, hold more than 2 people, survive a Canadian winter, keep the water clean without the host doing anything, and tell someone remotely when it needs attention. Joolca does none of that.

The gap Joolca leaves is the automation and the always-ready part, not the propane heater. Lucas's home setup already matches Joolca's hardware at the same price with room for 6, so the hardware alone is not where we would beat it.
