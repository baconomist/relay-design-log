# Cold weather and heating speed

16 Oct 2026

Lucas's 6-person SaluSpa can barely keep up on its electric heater at 0°C. This note estimates why, whether a tub can be used at -10°C, and how much faster propane heats.

- **With the cover off, the SaluSpa's 1.36 kW electric heater can't keep up below about 18°C**, even in calm air with 4 people in the tub covering part of the water. With nobody in it, or in a light breeze, it can't keep up anywhere from -10°C to 20°C. An open tub loses about 2 to 5 kW, mostly to evaporation.
- **With the cover on, it keeps up**, but only gains about 0.7°C an hour at -10°C, so it recovers slowly after a soak.
- **A cheap 6 L/min propane shower heater (41,000 BTU class) keeps up easily at -10°C with the cover off.** It puts about 9.6 kW into the tub, three to four times what an open tub loses at -10°C, so the water still warms 4 to 7°C an hour. A HOTTAP (about 5.4 kW into the tub) keeps up in calm air and just breaks even in a breeze.
- **Propane heats about 7 to 9 times faster.** Filling and heating the 908 L SaluSpa from 5°C takes about 30 to 37 hours on the electric heater, about 4.7 hours on a HOTTAP and about 3.9 hours on a 41,000 BTU shower heater.
- **-10°C is the practical limit anyway.** Bestway says not to run the spa below -10°C. Propane heaters must be drained near freezing, and a 20 lb tank struggles to supply a 37,500 BTU heater much below -15°C.

All heat-loss figures are own calc using the inputs and assumptions below. None have been measured yet.

| --- | --- |
| 4 people in, calm | **About 18°C** |

So with the cover off, the electric heater only keeps up on a warm summer evening. At 0°C with people in, the model says the water drops about 0.5°C an hour with the heater running, which fits "barely keeps up".

**Cover on**, the heater keeps up at every temperature in the table: it gains about 0.7°C an hour at -10°C, 0.85 at 0°C and 1.0 at 10°C. That uses the one owner-measured inflatable heat loss in 03 (about 11.8 W per °C), so it is weak.

## Propane keeps up where electric can't

A HOTTAP-type heater can't put more heat into the tub than its water flow allows. It heats water to at most 50°C ([Joolca](https://joolca.com/products/hottub-bundle)), and Joolca's pump moves up to 6 L/min ([Joolca 12V pump](https://www.joolca.com/products/12v-pump)). With tub water returning at about 37°C, that is 6 L/min heated by 13°C, or about **5.4 kW into the tub** (own calc). That is 4 times the electric heater and below the burner's limit of about 8.8 kW of useful heat.

Water temperature change with the cover off and a 5.4 kW propane heater running:

| Air °C | Empty, calm | 4 people, calm | Empty, breeze |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| -10 | +2.1°C/h | +3.1°C/h | +0.1°C/h |
| 0 | +2.4°C/h | +3.3°C/h | +0.6°C/h |
| 10 | +2.8°C/h | +3.6°C/h | +1.2°C/h |
| 20 | +3.3°C/h | +3.9°C/h | +2.0°C/h |

It only falls behind with the tub empty, in a breeze, below about -13°C. A pump with more flow raises the heat the heater can deliver, up to the burner's limit.

### A 6 L/min shower heater does better

Lucas pointed to a [6 L/min (1.58 GPM) outdoor propane water heater on Amazon.ca](https://www.amazon.ca/dp/B0F3BPXB6T), sold by FanmiTechnology. Its listing page was too large to read its specs, so the figures below are from other 1.58 GPM, 6 L heaters, which are rated **41,000 BTU/h** with about a 3 PSI start pressure and 2 D-cells for ignition ([VXB](https://vxb.com/products/portable-propane-tankless-water-heater-for-rv-and), [Camplux BD158 Pro](https://wayfair.com/home-improvement/pdp/camplux-pro-outdoor-158-gpm-liquid-propane-tankless-water-heatert-cmpx1070.html), [Gasland BE158](https://carrefouruae.com/mafuae/en/heater/gasland-tankless-water-heater-outdoors-be158l-158gpm-6l-outdoor-portable-gas-water-heater-propane-water-heater-overheating-protection-easy-to-install-use-for-rv-cabin-barn-camping-boat-blue/p/3233001419349), medium, retailer listings). One listing says 31,000 BTU, so check the actual unit's label.

41,000 BTU/h is 12 kW of propane. At 80% efficiency that puts about **9.6 kW** into the water, 7 times the SaluSpa's electric heater (own calc). At 6 L/min with 37°C water coming back from the tub, the water leaves the heater at about 60°C.

| Air °C | Empty, calm | 4 people, calm | Empty, breeze |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| -10 | **+6.0°C/h** | **+7.1°C/h** | **+4.1°C/h** |
| 0 | +6.4°C/h | +7.3°C/h | +4.6°C/h |
| 10 | +6.8°C/h | +7.6°C/h | +5.2°C/h |
| 20 | +7.3°C/h | +7.9°C/h | +6.0°C/h |

It keeps up with the cover off at -10°C in every case, even in a 4 m/s wind (about 8 kW of loss). At full burn it uses about 1.7 L of propane an hour, so a 20 lb tank lasts about 10 hours at full burn, about C$1.45 an hour. It only burns at full rate while catching up, so a real soak uses much less: about C$0.33 an hour at -10°C with 4 people in calm air (own calc).

Things to watch with this heater:
- **Water coming out at about 60°C** could scald someone sitting by the return jet. The return needs to mix into the tub, or the heater's output needs limiting.
- **Flow and pressure.** It needs about 6 L/min through it and about 3 PSI to light. The pump has to supply that.
- **The 20 lb tank in the cold.** 41,000 BTU/h is close to what a 20 lb tank can supply at -10°C (about 46,000 BTU/h on a full tank, less as it empties, interpolated from [Flame Engineering](https://flameengineering.com/pages/propane-information)). Two tanks joined together avoid running short.
- **Freezing.** Like all of these heaters, it has to be drained, or kept circulating warm water, whenever it is below 0°C.

## Heating speed

Heating the full 908 L tub from 5°C to 38.9°C takes about **35.8 kWh** of heat (own calc).

| Heater | Heat into tub | Time from 5°C, cover on, at -10°C / 0°C / 10°C outside |
| --- | --- | --- |
| SaluSpa electric, 1.36 kW | 1.36 kW minus losses | **37 / 33 / 30 hours** |
| HOTTAP-type propane at 6 L/min | About 5.4 kW | **About 4.7 hours** |
| 6 L/min shower heater, 41,000 BTU | About 9.6 kW | **About 3.9 hours** |

That makes propane **about 7 to 9 times faster**. Bestway SaluSpa manuals give 23 to 40 hours to heat from 10°C in 10°C weather and a heating rate of 1 to 1.5°C an hour ([Bestway manual](https://manuals.plus/bestway/saluspa-inflatable-hot-tub-manual), medium, seen in search snippets because the manual pages would not open), which matches the electric estimate. Joolca says its smaller 300 L tub heats in under 2 hours ([Joolca](https://joolca.com/products/hottub-bundle)).

**Propane cost at -10°C** (C$15 per 20 lb tank, about 100 kWh of heat per tank, see 03): heating the tub from 5°C uses about a third of a tank, about **C$5.40**. An hour-long soak with 4 people in calm air uses about 2.2 kWh, about **C$0.33** (own calc).

## How the heat loss was worked out

| Input | Value | Source and strength |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tub | SaluSpa Hollywood, 196 x 66 cm outside, **908 L** | [BMR](https://www.bmr.ca/en/bestway-saluspa-hollywood-airjet-77-x-26-1-96-m-x-66-cm-001-6875.html), medium. Lucas's exact model not confirmed |
| Open water area | **1.65 m²** empty, **1.05 m²** with 4 people in (about 0.15 m² each) | Own estimate: 908 L at about 55 cm deep. Measure the real tub |
| Electric heater | 11.3 A at 120 V, about **1.36 kW**. The whole unit is rated 12 A | [Bestway manual](https://a.sellpoint.net/a/vYM8Rxyo.pdf), medium, from search snippets because the PDF was too large to open. 1.36 kW heating 908 L with no losses is 1.3°C an hour, matching the manual's 1 to 1.5°C an hour |
| Evaporation | Evaporation coefficient (25 + 19 x air speed) kg/m²h, times the difference in humidity between saturated air at the water's temperature and the outdoor air. Outdoor humidity 75% | [Engineering ToolBox](https://www.engineeringtoolbox.com/evaporation-water-surface-d_690.html), medium. Empirical, made for pools |
| Convection | Taken as a fixed share of evaporation using the Bowen ratio (0.0665 kPa/K x temperature difference ÷ vapour pressure difference) | Textbook relation |
| Radiation | Water surface radiating to surroundings at air temperature, emissivity 0.95 | Textbook. A clear night sky is colder than the air, so real loss is higher |
| Walls and floor | 6 W per °C | Assumption, about half the owner-measured cover-on loss in 03 |

Evaporation is about half of the total loss, and convection about a quarter. So **covering the water is what matters most**. Bubbles push cold outside air through the water and would make cover-off losses worse. They are not included.

## What this means for the design

- **Use each heater for what it's good at.** Electricity is cheaper for holding temperature with the cover on (see 03). Propane is the only practical way to heat fast and keep up with the cover off in winter. A controller that holds temperature on electricity and switches on propane for heat-up and soaks gets both.
- **Size the pump for the propane heater.** Heat delivered is flow times temperature rise. A bigger pump means more heat, up to the burner's limit.
- **Protect the propane heater from freezing.** It must drain itself, or keep warm tub water circulating, whenever it is below 0°C.
- **Keep the cover on.** An automated or easy-to-close cover, or a reminder when it's left open, saves more energy than any heater choice. A windbreak helps too: a 2 m/s breeze adds about 65% to the loss.

## Test this on Lucas's tub

1. Measure the inside diameter and water depth to get the real area and volume.
2. On a cold night, log the water temperature every 10 minutes with the cover off and the heater on, and note the air temperature and wind. The slope gives the real heat loss to check against the table.
3. Do the same with the propane heater, and weigh the tank before and after to get its real efficiency.
